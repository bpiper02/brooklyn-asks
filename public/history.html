TEN YEARS OF WHAT WOULDN’T GO AWAY

Which neighborhood problems kept coming back?

A single request can be easy to ignore. The same issue appearing again and again is harder to miss. Explore a decade of what Brooklyn neighborhoods formally raised — and see where the pattern starts to repeat.

WHY LOOK ACROSS YEARS?

Because persistence changes the story.

Brooklyn Asks connects scattered annual records into one timeline. That makes it easier to spot recurring flooding, transit, park, school, housing, safety and infrastructure concerns instead of reading each year in isolation.

READ THIS CAREFULLY

Coming back is a signal, not a verdict.

01

Formal neighborhood record

These records capture issues Community Boards formally raised with the city. They do not represent every concern residents had.

02

No invented outcomes

If a historical source does not include an agency response or verified outcome, Brooklyn Asks does not infer one.

03

Persistence needs context

The same issue returning can reflect an unresolved problem, a long project, recurring maintenance, or changing scope. Open the source before drawing stronger conclusions.
